# Chapter 4.3: asset deployment

Generated by `tzbank.chapters.ch04.deployment` (stage `profitability`). Blueprint §4, 4.3; hypothesis H4.2.

## Research question

Where does each bank put its balance sheet, and at what yields: loans versus government securities and placements,
and which sectors and segments does the loan book serve?

## Variable definitions

Loan yield = interest income on loans (note line) / average gross loans; securities yield = interest on
government securities / average government securities; IEA yield = interest income / average interest-earning
assets; asset mix = loans (net), securities, placements, IEA over total assets.

## Limitations

- Government securities and placements are held only from FY2020 (inputs workbook), so their yields start then.
- Loans by segment and by economic sector: 2 bank(s), 11 year(s) reconciled
- Yields mix stage-3 and performing loans (IFRS 9 interest on net stage-3 balances from FY2018).

## Interpretation

**Interpretation.** Differences in loan yield and in the securities share are the deployment leg of H4.2; 4.0 measures their weight in the ROE gap (asset yield, IEA/TA).

## Cross-cutting explanations of rent (blueprint §0)

High loan yields on a retail/government-salary book bear on **distribution and market access** and **customer
switching costs**; a large securities share bears on **regulatory advantages** and the sovereign carry, and
**temporary credit-cycle conditions** (yield versus impairment, 4.6).
