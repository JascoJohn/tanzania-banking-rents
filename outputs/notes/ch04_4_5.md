# Chapter 4.5: operating leverage

Generated by `tzbank.chapters.ch04.operating_leverage` (stage `profitability`). Blueprint §4, 4.5; hypothesis H4.2;
§0 scale economies.

## Research question

Does each bank convert growth into profit through operating leverage, and does it show scale economies?

## Variable definitions

Cost-to-income = OpEx / (NII + non-interest income); OpEx / average assets (equal to the 4.0 term opex_ta, checked
in the tests); incremental margin = (Delta income - Delta OpEx) / Delta income; scale elasticity = slope of log OpEx on
log average assets (OLS per bank, 95% interval); per-employee, per-branch and per-agent ratios use the KPIs (MEDIUM
confidence, +/-10% bands); staff cost per employee = staff costs / employees.

## Results (Derived)

- CRDB FY2025: cost-to-income 41.9%, OpEx / avg assets 4.38%; mean incremental margin 53.0% (median).
  Scale elasticity 2010-2025: 0.92 (95% CI 0.78-1.06): no evidence of scale economies (CI includes or exceeds 1).
  Profit per employee FY2024: TZS 130m (band 118-144m; MEDIUM).
- NMB FY2025: cost-to-income 37.1%, OpEx / avg assets 4.32%; mean incremental margin 60.8% (median).
  Scale elasticity 2009-2025: 0.81 (95% CI 0.72-0.91): scale economies (CI below 1).
  Profit per employee FY2025: TZS 186m (band 169-207m; MEDIUM).

## Evidence class

Tables: **Derived**; KPI ratios MEDIUM confidence. Statements under *Draft interpretation* are **Interpretation**.

## Limitations

- Elasticity regressions use 10-17 annual points per bank; intervals are wide and the slope mixes scale with
  inflation and investment cycles (descriptive).
- KPIs are sparse and MEDIUM confidence. Staff costs are the Group employee-benefit totals (12 values corrected in
  the pre-flight checks: CRDB note totals, NMB income-statement line).
- Incremental margins are unstable in years when income barely moves.

## Interpretation

**Interpretation.** Falling OpEx / assets was the largest single contributor to both banks' ROE gains from 2019 to 2025 in the 4.0 attribution; the elasticities say whether that is scale (cost growing more slowly than assets) or a cyclical squeeze.

## Cross-cutting explanations of rent (blueprint §0)

An elasticity below 1 with a falling OpEx / assets supports **scale economies**; it does not by itself separate scale
from **limited competition** (pricing power keeps income growing while costs lag).
