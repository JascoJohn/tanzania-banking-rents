## Part I. Architecture of dominance

### Chapter 1. Concentration and market position

**Central question.** How concentrated is Tanzanian banking, and how dominant are NMB and CRDB in the banking system and on the DSE?

**Primary output.** Asset, deposit, loan, profit and DSE concentration over time

**Hypotheses**

- H1.1 NMB + CRDB's share of sector profit exceeds their share of sector assets persistently, not only in 2026.
- H1.2 Their share of DSE market value and turnover is larger than their share of the banking system.

**Test.** CR2 by assets, deposits, loans and profit, annually. Profit share minus asset share as a rent indicator. DSE shares of market cap and turnover.

**Data held / needed.** Held: bank-level history, DSE data. Needed: BoT sector totals by year; DSE market cap and turnover history.

**Achievable strength.** Strong once sector totals are collected

### Chapter 2. Credit allocation patterns

**Central question.** How do the two banks' loan books differ from each other and from the system?

**Primary output.** Sectoral and borrower-type comparisons

**Hypotheses**

- H2.1 The two banks' sector loan mixes differ materially from each other.
- H2.2 Their combined loan mix differs from the banking system's.

**Test.** Sector shares of each loan book (annual-report concentration notes) against BoT credit by economic activity; dissimilarity index.

**Data held / needed.** Held: annual-report sector notes (to extract). Needed: BoT credit to the private sector by activity.

**Achievable strength.** Moderate, descriptive only; no economy-wide causal claim

### Chapter 3. Systemic importance

**Central question.** What benefits and constraints come with institutional importance?

**Primary output.** Capital buffers, regulatory requirements, funding advantage

**Hypotheses**

- H3.1 Systemic importance carries a funding advantage (lower deposit cost) that is offset in part by higher capital requirements.
- H3.2 Valuation multiples do not show a premium for systemic importance.

**Test.** Verify whether a BoT D-SIB framework and designations exist, and their dates. Capital buffer above requirement. Cost of deposits against smaller panel banks. P/B against ROE across the panel and peers.

**Data held / needed.** Held: capital history, panel funding data. Needed: BoT D-SIB guidelines and designations (verify), capital requirements history.

**Achievable strength.** Moderate; depends on what the regulation provides

## Part II. Economics of banking profits

### Chapter 4. The economics of banking profits and business models

**Central question.** How does each bank make money, and why do their economics differ?

**Primary output.** Seven-part business-model analysis and comparative business-model map

**Hypotheses**

- H4.1 Most of NMB's and CRDB's excess return comes from their funding advantage, not lending margin or fees.
- H4.2 The two banks' excess returns rest on different profit engines.

**Test.** Seven subsections (table below), ending in the comparative business-model map.

**Data held / needed.** Held: full NMB/CRDB history; seven-bank panel; NPS payments panel. Needed: T-bill history; fee and loan-sector notes (extract).

**Achievable strength.** Strong

**Subsections**

| 4.1 | Revenue architecture | Interest and non-interest income | Income composition, growth, volatility |

### Chapter 5. Persistence and economic rents

**Central question.** How fast do excess returns fade?

**Primary output.** Persistence coefficients and half-lives with confidence intervals

**Hypotheses**

- H5.1 Excess returns are persistent (λ well above zero) but mean-reverting (λ < 1).
- H5.2 Long-run rent ER\* is positive for NMB and CRDB and near zero or negative for the smaller panel banks.
- H5.3 Persistence is higher for banks with a stronger net deposit franchise.

**Test.** AR(1) as descriptive benchmark; structural-break tests; alternative specifications; bootstrap intervals; COE ±200 bp; peer benchmark.

**Data held / needed.** Held: seven-bank panel, about 17 years. Needed: annual COE inputs (auction history from 2012; earlier years from BoT); peer data (extension).

**Achievable strength.** Moderate: few annual observations per bank; results stated with intervals and validity rules

### Chapter 6. Funding architecture

**Central question.** What makes the deposit franchise valuable?

**Primary output.** Cost of deposits, deposit mix, deposit beta

**Hypotheses**

- H6.1 NMB and CRDB have a lower cost of deposits, lower deposit beta and larger net deposit franchise contribution than the smaller banks.
- H6.2 Loan growth outrunning deposits raises wholesale funding reliance and compresses margin.

**Test.** Cost of deposits; deposit mix; deposit beta; gross funding advantage and net deposit franchise contribution; LDR; borrowings share.

**Data held / needed.** Held: interest expense, deposits, borrowings. Needed: deposit-mix notes; T-bill, CBR and reserve-requirement history.

**Achievable strength.** Strong

### Chapter 7. Capital allocation

**Central question.** Does retained capital earn more than its cost?

**Primary output.** Return on retained earnings, incremental ROE

**Hypotheses**

- H7.1 Retained earnings have been reinvested at returns above COE for both banks.
- H7.2 Incremental returns are declining as the banks scale.
- H7.3 The banks differ in capital allocation (growth versus distribution).

**Test.** Primary RORE on attributable earnings and retained equity; incremental ROE (k = 3, 5); EPS-based RORE supplementary; payout; RWA versus equity growth; capital headroom.

**Data held / needed.** Held: full history. Needed: none.

**Achievable strength.** Strong (descriptive, not causal)

### Chapter 8. Credit risk and capital efficiency

**Central question.** How efficiently is risk capital deployed through the cycle?

**Primary output.** Through-the-cycle cost of risk, return on RWA

**Hypotheses**

- H8.1 The current low cost of risk is below the through-the-cycle level.
- H8.2 Return on RWA differs between the banks after adjusting for credit cost.

**Test.** Cost of risk over the full cycle including 2015–2017; NPL and coverage history; return on RWA; IFRS 9 transition effect.

**Data held / needed.** Held: full history incl. IAS 39/IFRS 9 break. Needed: none.

**Achievable strength.** Strong

## Part III. Investment valuation

### Chapter 9. Comparative valuation

**Central question.** What are NMB and CRDB worth at both valuation dates?

**Primary output.** RIM and capital-constrained DDM

**Hypotheses**

- H9.1 Intrinsic values at the 31 Dec 2025 point-in-time date differ materially from the prices then.

**Test.** RIM 60% / capital-constrained DDM 40%; both valuation dates; cut-off policy enforced. Long-run ROE and fade from chapter 5.

**Data held / needed.** Held: inputs workbook v0.5. Needed: calibrated assumption register.

**Achievable strength.** Strong

### Chapter 10. Market-implied expectations

**Central question.** What persistence of rents does the price assume?

**Primary output.** Implied persistence and implied long-run ROE

**Hypotheses**

- H10.1 The 2026 rerating raised the persistence the market prices beyond what history supports.

**Test.** Iso-value curves of (λ, ER\*) at both dates against the chapter 5 estimates. Implied COE. Rerating decomposition: earnings, discount rate, residual.

**Data held / needed.** Held: prices, bond yields, engine. Needed: chapter 5 results.

**Achievable strength.** Strong; the central finding

### Chapter 11. Strategic divergence scenarios

**Central question.** How could the two banks' economics diverge?

**Primary output.** Scenario-based intrinsic values

**Hypotheses**

- H11.1 The banks' different capital-allocation and funding paths produce materially different value outcomes under the same macro scenario.

**Test.** Bear/base/bull built from chapter 6–8 evidence; Monte Carlo; tested historical call (31 Dec 2025 value versus 2026 outcome).

**Data held / needed.** Held: engine, history. Needed: none beyond calibration.

**Achievable strength.** Strong

### Chapter 12. USD returns and implementation

**Central question.** Can an institutional investor capture the return?

**Primary output.** USD returns, capacity, trading cost, market access

**Hypotheses**

- H12.1 After depreciation and trading costs, expected USD returns still exceed a USD hurdle in the base case.
- H12.2 Position capacity, not valuation, is the binding constraint for a large fund.

**Test.** USD translation and break-even depreciation; capacity at a % of daily traded value; trading-cost estimate; market access (CMSA, custody, tax, repatriation).

**Data held / needed.** Held: daily trading data, FX, liquidity metrics. Needed: market-access research with cited sources; DSE fee schedule.

**Achievable strength.** Strong for quantitative parts; access findings verified by counsel
